Activities and preparation

Statistical looking and sampling

Read four displays and decide what question each can answer. Then take a random sample of 25 shapes and estimate the proportion of circles.

Continue the investigation
Bring your sample record and explain why two groups can get different estimates from the same population.

One categorical variable and fair comparisons

Make a relative-frequency table and bar chart in ISLE. In the work and sleep Data Studio, decide which groups make sense to compare.

Continue the investigation
Write one sentence about your display. Bring the strongest reason for your choice of denominators.

Contingency tables

Build a shape-by-fill table from raw records, turn conditional percentages into a graph, and diagnose an ISLE display.

Continue the investigation
For work and money stress, show each count out of its own group total and compare the percentages in points.

Could chance explain the difference?

Predict what happens when 28 surviving penguins out of 100 are shuffled into two groups of 50. Compare the resulting differences with the observed 18/50 versus 10/50.

Continue the investigation
Explain what one shuffle represents and what the p-value measures in this example.

How big might the difference be?

Use the penguin example to build a bootstrap interval by resampling from each group separately.

Continue the investigation
Explain what happens to precision when sample size increases while the observed proportions stay the same.

When is the evidence convincing?

Compare evidence across studies. Use the point estimate, p-value, and confidence interval to answer three different questions.

Continue the investigation
For a stop-rate difference of 5 points with a 95% interval of −0.2 to 10.2 points, explain what both ends suggest.

    Michigan voter-turnout project

    Compare two assigned voter messages. Use the observed difference, a randomization result, and a bootstrap interval to report what you found.

    Continue the investigation
    Explain in plain language what was shuffled and what was resampled.

    Adding a third variable

    Revisit voter turnout within subgroups, then investigate a marital-status comparison in ACS data.

    Continue the investigation
    Show the overall comparison alongside the subgroup comparisons. Explain what the additional variable revealed.

    Who gets a callback?

    Use the Milwaukee audit to investigate the relationships among race, criminal record, and callbacks.

    Continue the investigation
    Make two two-variable comparisons and one three-variable comparison. Explain the finding you think is worth reporting.

    Job quality and subgroup comparisons

    Compare responses in the Gallup job-quality data across groups and then within a third variable.

    Continue the investigation
    Use group counts and conditional percentages to support one claim about what persists or changes.

    Describing numerical data

    Use Ten Numbers About Us to investigate a distribution. Describe its typical values, spread, shape, and any unusual values.

    Continue the investigation
    Choose one class-survey display and say what is worth sharing about it.

    • Data Studio: What Is Worth Saying About Our Class?
    Comparing numerical distributions

    Use grouped boxplots, overlaid or faceted histograms, and grouped summaries. Try a comparison with U.S. birth data.

    Continue the investigation
    Explain what the median and IQR each tell you about the two groups.

    • STAT C1000 | Standard display for comparing two groups
    Wages in Alameda County

    Explore education, age, and work class. Compare bachelor’s with master’s degrees and private with government workers. Then look within work class and bring in age.

    Continue the investigation
    Use the graphs and tables to explain a pattern in typical wages, spread, or the share earning over $150,000.

    The Texture of Student Life

    Choose a question prompted by a survey distribution. Build three pairwise comparisons and two follow-ups that add a third variable.

    Continue the investigation
    Share which comparison you would use for inference and why.

    • The Texture of Student Life — STAT C1000 Final Project
    Tell the story of your findings

    Bring your displays together into a short investigation. Explain how your comparisons developed and what the uncertainty leaves open.

    Continue the investigation
    Draft a one-page summary for an Ohlone student seeing the survey for the first time.
